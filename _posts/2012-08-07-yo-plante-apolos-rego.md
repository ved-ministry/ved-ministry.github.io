“I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth.” 1 Co. 3:6

In the past, when the church was begun in the Inca region, many were those who planted and worked hard. The apostle Paul said in 1 Co. 3:6, “I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth,” and in 2 Ti. 2:6, “It is the hard-working farmer who ought to have the first share of the crops.” But as time went by, the work of the planters became harder and harder, because the work was growing in a natural and spontaneous way through the labor of the believers themselves in different places. Now we are living in these times of growth and spontaneity, and for the new believers and works it is almost hard to keep moving forward, for many reasons.

Number one

The lack of prepared workers with a passion for service, who are not seeking dishonest gain.

Number two

Ministries that focus on the people and lives of the more distant places, such as the countryside.

Number three

Help from mature brothers and churches who can invest their time and resources to keep equipping new leaders in their areas.

ALL IN HIS NAME AND FOR HIM

In the EPYL ministry we have accepted and taken up this challenge. We have only the desire to seek the spiritual well-being and maturity of the new leaders without taking them away from their geographic and living context.

This model is a reminder of the apostle Paul: “I planted, Apollos watered, but God gave the growth” (1 Co. 3:6). When the goal is spiritual fruit for the glory of God, who is more important? The one who planted, or the one who watered? Or who teaches? Who will give for this work? God is the one who gives and sustains his work by his grace alone.

AMEN.
