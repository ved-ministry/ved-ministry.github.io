This is for lack of a head! This is Hosea’s fundamental accusation. The nation ignores the law of God. Fornication and wine took away good sense and good judgment.

The kingdom of Jeroboam II enjoyed political prestige and economic prosperity: “…with their silver and gold they made idols for themselves, that they might be cut off” (8:4). Ephraim said: “Ah, but I am rich; I have found wealth for myself; in all my labors they cannot find in me iniquity or sin” (12:8). Historical evidence shows this as Israel’s richest period, thanks above all to trade in olive oil, wine and wheat with both Egypt and Assyria.

Whoredom, wine and new wine take away the understanding (4:11). Their drink is gone; they give themselves to whoring; their rulers dearly love shame (4:18). They do not cry to me from the heart, but they wail on their beds; for grain and wine they gather themselves, they rebel against me (7:14). Rejoice not, O Israel! Exult not like the peoples; for you have played the whore, forsaking your God. You have loved a prostitute’s wages on all threshing floors (9:1). Threshing floor and winepress shall not feed them, and the new wine shall fail them (9:2). Ephraim feeds on the wind and pursues the east wind all day long; they multiply falsehood and violence; they make a covenant with Assyria, and oil is carried to Egypt (12:1).

Hosea’s message is current for us and calls us to reflect: how should we act in times of prosperity? Is God still calling his servants to serve him? The God of ancient history is the God who works in modern history.

Internal corruption is more dangerous than external enemies.

Missionary: Cristóbal Ch.

Serving the Master in Cusco, Peru
