> And a letter came to him from Elijah the prophet, saying: Thus says the LORD, the God of David your father: Because you have not walked in the ways of Jehoshaphat your father, or in the ways of Asa king of Judah, (2 Chr. 21:12)

Sometimes I heard that the prophet Elijah wrote nothing, but we have God’s testimony that Elijah wrote a letter to a king named Jehoram.

The prophet Elijah wrote a letter to a king named Jehoram, son of Jehoshaphat. What was the purpose of this letter? 1. Because you have not walked in the ways of Jehoshaphat your father, or in the ways of Asa king of Judah (v. 12b). Jehoram killed all his brothers and also some of the princes of Israel (v. 4). He married a daughter of Ahab, and did what was evil in the sight of the LORD (v. 5). He made high places for idolatrous worship so that the inhabitants of Jerusalem would prostitute themselves after them (v. 11). A son can be raised with good principles and examples, but if his heart is full of idolatry and rebellion, his perdition and his own choice are his ruin. There are many children growing up with this wickedness in their hearts.

Idolatry, open rebellion against God, blinds good judgment.

2. Judgment from God (vv. 13-20). God sent him his message through the prophet Elijah. This message confronted King Jehoram so that he would turn from his evil ways and serve God. When a man’s heart hardens, God withdraws like a perfect gentleman; there was no need to insist, Jehoram chose his own way. What was God’s judgment on Jehoram? God would strike his people with a great plague. The Lord would also strike his children, his wives, and all that he had. The greatest judgment would be for Jehoram himself: many diseases. Jehoram spent many days sick, for two years, until his bowels came out because of the many diseases. God kept his promise, and so we see the fulfillment of his word.

Many times illnesses are the consequence of not having a spiritual life of communion and obedience to God.

God’s words are just judgments for believers. In obeying them are their delights and blessings. It is God’s message for every disciple.
